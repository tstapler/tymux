# Adversarial Review: attach-resume-protocol

**Date**: 2026-08-24
**Re-reviewed**: 2026-08-24 (repair-loop re-review, both blockers below; engineering repair pass re-review of the cross-client/Phase-6 Concerns below)
**Verdict**: CONCERNS (both blockers resolved; of the 3 original Concerns, 1 is now resolved and 1 is narrowed — see Concerns section; 2 Concerns remain open)

## Blockers

- [x] **RESOLVED** — `allocate_replay_budget`'s planned "mirror `allocate_scrollback_budget` exactly" instruction inherited a formula bug that defeated the global replay-buffer ceiling. *(Original finding, verified at `crates/tymux-core/src/pane.rs:44-49`: `allocate_scrollback_budget` computes `granted = DEFAULT.min(remaining.max(MIN))`, always granting at least `MIN` even once the global budget is exhausted.)* **Re-verified 2026-08-24**: `pane.rs:44-49` is unchanged (correctly — that formula stays intentional for scrollback). The fix is in plan.md, not pane.rs: Task 2.1.1d (`plan.md:284-291`) now explicitly instructs "Do not copy `allocate_scrollback_budget`'s grant formula... verbatim" and specifies a genuine hard-cap formula instead — `let remaining = GLOBAL_REPLAY_BUFFER_BUDGET_BYTES.saturating_sub(used); let granted = DEFAULT_REPLAY_BUFFER_BYTES.min(remaining);` — with no `.max(MIN_REPLAY_BUFFER_BYTES)` floor, so `granted` can legitimately fall to `0` once the global budget is exhausted. The Pattern Decisions table (`plan.md:87`) and glossary (`plan.md:54`) both consistently state the same distinction and correctly justify why scrollback keeps its floor (copy-mode needs *some* history) while replay buffer doesn't (a `0`-byte pane safely falls back to `GapExceeded`, per Epic 2.2.2). This closes the arithmetic defeat: `GLOBAL_REPLAY_BUFFER_BUDGET_BYTES` is now a genuine ceiling, not a soft target.

- [x] **RESOLVED** — plan.md's stated cut order previously widened requirements.md's authorized cut ("Phase 6 cut first as a whole" vs. requirements.md's narrower "(1) drop tymux-cli's cross-invocation persistence first"), which would have left `tymux-cli` with zero reconnect capability under schedule pressure. **Re-verified 2026-08-24** against requirements.md's Appetite section verbatim (`requirements.md:114-129`): "(1) drop `tymux-cli`'s cross-invocation persistence first — ship the protocol + reference-client test coverage, defer CLI reconnect to a follow-up; (2) if still overrun, fall back to the Minimal scope from the original ideation pass... defer the dedicated replay buffer." plan.md's Dependency Visualization (`plan.md:169-174`) now states the cut order as two numbered steps matching this exactly: step 1 cuts "Epic 6.2 only" (cross-invocation persistence), explicitly keeping Epic 6.1 (in-process reconnect loop, needs no persistence) and Epic 6.3 (UX polish) in scope; step 2 falls back to Minimal scope, deferring the replay buffer (Epics 2.1/2.2/2.3). Phase 6's Framing paragraph (`plan.md:569`) and Epic 6.2's heading/Goal (`plan.md:596`) were updated consistently. plan.md also transparently flags its own residual interpretive judgment call (the "Open note" at `plan.md:174`, on whether requirements.md's "defer CLI reconnect to a follow-up" phrase means only Epic 6.2 or the whole `tymux-cli` loop) rather than silently picking a reading — this is a reasonable, disclosed interpretation, not a new gap. The cut order no longer risks leaving `tymux-cli` at zero reconnect capability under the first cut tier.

## Concerns

- [x] **RESOLVED (engineering repair pass, 2026-08-24; re-confirmed 2026-08-24).** Heartbeat delivery during a long replay-drain — originally flagged as unaddressed by either epic that touches it. Now explicitly resolved: Story 2.3.1's acceptance criteria (`plan.md:384-385`) state as a requirement, not just an inferred glossary implication, that the replay-drain loop deliberately does NOT gain its own `heartbeat_interval` branch, because the client-side idle timer (Task 6.1.1d) resets on *any* received event — `OutputChunk` included, not `Heartbeat` alone — so a steady stream of legitimate replay chunks already counts as proof-of-life. Story 3.2.1's own text (`plan.md:390`) cross-references this decision in the other direction ("Do NOT add a `heartbeat_interval` branch to this loop — see Story 2.3.1's acceptance criteria for why"), so the two epics are explicitly reconciled, not independently silent. Task 6.1.1e's test double (`plan.md:693`) sends `OutputChunk` events on a cadence slower than `heartbeat_interval` (15s) but within `heartbeat_timeout` (45s) and asserts no spurious reconnect — converting the "any event counts" semantics from an assumption into a tested requirement.
- [ ] **NARROWED (engineering repair pass, 2026-08-24) — partially mitigated, not resolved. The Success Metric "heartbeat distinguishing slow vs. gone" has no task, in any non-cut-candidate phase, that actually consumes a `Heartbeat` event or exercises `heartbeat_timeout`/backoff logic.** Epic 5.1/5.2's ACs (clients/ts, clients/go) cover only `resumeFromSeq` and `GapExceeded` — not heartbeat consumption. That logic is currently tasked only under Phase 6 (`tymux-cli`), which is also the first (Blocker #2) or entire (per plan.md) cut candidate. If Phase 6 is cut in whole or in part, no client anywhere exercises the heartbeat/backoff half of "one shared spec, implemented identically by all three clients" — it stays documentation-only.

  **What's changed**: two things reduce, but don't eliminate, this Concern's force. (1) Blocker #2 above (already resolved) clarifies the actual cut order is finer-grained than "cut Phase 6 as a whole": only Epic 6.2 (cross-invocation persistence) is the *first* thing cut; Epic 6.1 (the in-process reconnect loop, where `heartbeat_timeout`/backoff logic actually runs — Tasks 6.1.1b/6.1.1d) stays in scope even under that first cut tier, and even the deeper fallback (requirements.md step 2, Minimal scope) explicitly retains "heartbeat/grace-period" as in-scope feature work — so the "Phase 6 is cut in whole" scenario this Concern originally worried about is a narrower, less likely risk than stated. (2) plan.md's new Task 4.1.2a (`plan.md:554-564`, Story 4.1.2) adds a per-client conformance test verifying each client's backoff CONSTANTS match ADR-004 (revised)'s numbers.

  **What remains unaddressed**: Task 4.1.2a is a constants-only equality check, not a runtime-behavior test — it says so explicitly ("a flat constant-equality check per client, not a shared library or cross-language test runner"). It exercises no actual reconnect loop, no real `heartbeat_timeout` firing, and no real backoff delay in any client. `clients/ts` and `clients/go` (Epics 5.1/5.2) never implement a full reconnect loop at all — their Task 4.1.2a obligation is only to define and assert against a backoff-constants module, not to consume `Heartbeat` events or retry on disconnect. The only place actual heartbeat/backoff *runtime behavior* is exercised anywhere in this plan is `tymux-cli`'s Epic 6.1 (Tasks 6.1.1d/6.1.1e). Narrowed statement of what's left open: constants are now verified to match across all three clients, but only `tymux-cli`'s actual runtime reconnect/heartbeat-timeout behavior is tested anywhere — `clients/ts`/`clients/go`'s "compliance" with the shared spec is constants-only, not behavior-verified, independent of whether Epic 6.1 itself ever gets cut.

- [x] **RESOLVED (engineering repair pass, 2026-08-24). No cross-client conformance check verifies the three implementations of the shared backoff/heartbeat numbers (ADR-004, revised: 200ms/x2/8s-cap/±20%/14-attempts) actually agree with each other.** Each client is tested independently against the server (Epics 5.1, 5.2, 6.1), never against the proto doc-comment spec text or against one another. A transcription drift (e.g. one client shipping a different jitter percentage) would pass every listed test and ship undetected.

  **Resolution**: plan.md's new Story 4.1.2 / Task 4.1.2a (`plan.md:554-564`) adds exactly this check: a per-client test in `tymux-cli` (Task 6.1.1b's helper), `clients/ts` (`clients/ts/test/backoff.test.ts`), and `clients/go` (`clients/go/backoff_test.go`), each hardcoding ADR-004 (revised)'s five numbers (200ms / x2 / 8s / +/-20% / 14 attempts) and asserting equality against that client's own exported backoff constants. A transcription drift in any one client's constants — the exact failure mode this Concern named — now fails that client's own test suite, naming the mismatched constant and ADR-004's expected value. This resolves the Concern as written (agreement between the three implementations' *stated constants* and the spec, catching drift). See the Concern above for the separate, narrower question of whether those constants are actually exercised by real reconnect/backoff *behavior* in every client — Task 4.1.2a does not claim to cover that, and doesn't.

## Minors

- Replay-buffer sizing (256KiB default / 16KiB min / 64MiB global) means most panes beyond roughly the 256th concurrent pane fall to the 16KiB floor (~4 PTY reads, `PTY_READ_BUF_SIZE=4096` at `pane.rs:60`) at the 1,000-session load-test scale — a thin resume window for a busy pane under exactly the load NFRs cite. Already labeled "informed default, not measured" (Task 2.1.1d, requirements.md Feasibility Risks), so this is residual risk on an already-acknowledged unknown, not a new gap.
- `resume_from_seq` interacting with `Pane::spawn_with_id`/session revival (daemon-restart survival, explicitly out of scope) is safely handled by construction — a stale token from before a revive is simply larger than the fresh pane's low `output_seq`, so `replay_since` correctly falls into `GapExceeded` (per the plan's own "`resume_from_seq > latest_seq` → `GapExceeded`" rule) — but no test case names this scenario explicitly.
- Task 3.2.2b ("guard against window already closed") is the one place ADR-003 admits defensive code is still needed, but neither ADR-003 nor plan.md specifies the detection mechanism (an `Option`/`Result` from `unregister_viewport`, or a pre-check). Low risk since the need is already flagged, just underspecified.

## Verified against the actual codebase (not taken on the plan's citations alone)

- `output_seq: AtomicU64` is confirmed 1-indexed (`fetch_add(1,...) + 1` at `pane.rs:249`), bumped inside the same `parser` lock guard that processes the chunk, then read again outside the lock after it's dropped, before `output_tx.send` — matches the plan's stated seq/locking model exactly (`pane.rs:246-255`).
- The Ctrl-d/blocked-forever precedent cited by `research/pitfalls.md` §5 is real: `docs/reviews/is-it-ready-2026-07-13.md` documents 5 of 7 independent reviewers converging on the same root cause (forwarding task blocking forever on `output_rx.recv()` with no exit signal). Epic 2.3's per-chunk `wait_exit()` race during replay-drain is a directly on-point mitigation for reintroducing this exact bug class.
- `client_id = self.engine.new_client_id()` is confirmed freshly minted on every `Attach` call (`main.rs:650`), substantiating ADR-003's rejection of a cancelable per-`client_id` grace-period timer ("nothing to cancel").
- `disconnect_tracker: Arc<Mutex<HashMap<Uuid, Instant>>>` is confirmed keyed by `pane_id` only, not per-client (`main.rs:47`), substantiating ADR-002's stated rationale for rejecting a server-tracked `HashMap<ClientId, Cursor>` resume design.
- `clients/go/v0.1.0` is confirmed as the only existing tag (`git tag -l`), consistent with ADR-001's claim that stapler-squad is pinned there today and the breaking `OutputChunk` wire change won't reach it without a deliberate `v0.2.0` bump.
- The exact "production `Server::builder()` only, not the 2 test-helper sites" claim (Story 3.1.1) is precisely correct: 3 call sites total in `main.rs` (line 947, before `#[cfg(test)] mod tests` at line 991; lines 1030 and 1658, both inside it).
- `crates/tymux-cli/src/main.rs:544`'s `None => break AttachOutcome::Done` is confirmed as the literal no-reconnect-loop-at-all baseline Phase 6's framing describes.
